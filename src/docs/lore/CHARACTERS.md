# Bunbit Character & Gameplay Feedback Compilation

**Collected community impressions – July 2026**

---

# Purpose

This document compiles early feedback regarding:

* Character designs (Patches & Vesper)
* Art direction
* Gameplay philosophy
* Collaboration advice
* Development priorities

The goal is **not** to vote on a "correct" design, but to identify recurring themes and useful insights before moving into production with an artist.

---

# Jamazu

## Character impressions

### Pixel-art concept

* Reads clearly as pixel art.
* Lacks environmental mood because the characters are isolated.
* Better for readability than atmosphere.

### Detailed anime illustration

Strengths:

* Excellent depth.
* Rich detail.
* Strong atmosphere.
* Highly readable silhouettes.

Weaknesses:

* Mechanic outfit feels derivative of existing games.
* Risks feeling less unique.

### Middle concept

* Feels like AI averaging the two styles.
* Interesting mood.
* Needs significantly more refinement.
* Doesn't yet feel like a finished artistic direction.

---

## General observations

The contrast between Patches and Vesper communicates immediately.

The personalities are readable even without context.

However:

* Cute characters placed inside a dark world create an intentionally unsettling atmosphere.

This was noticed immediately.

---

## Clothing observations

Business clothing raised questions.

If shirts/ties have lore:

* Great.

If not:

* They may confuse players.

The clothing should reinforce worldbuilding instead of existing purely for appearance.

---

## Collaboration advice

Probably the most valuable advice:

> Let the artist take ownership.

Instead of endlessly generating AI iterations:

* Build references.
* Present your vision.
* Then allow the artist to interpret it.

If the artist becomes excited about one direction...

That excitement will likely produce the best work.

---

## Development philosophy

Jamazu strongly suggested:

Trust the Muse.

Trust collaborators.

Don't over-polish AI concepts.

Let human creativity begin as early as possible.

---

## Personal takeaway

Main lessons:

* Art is subjective.
* Your collaborators are specialists.
* Your job is direction—not controlling every pixel.

# Repeating themes across everyone

Several observations appeared multiple times.

## 1. Personalities are immediately readable

Everyone interpreted them differently—

—but nobody struggled to distinguish them.

That is a very positive sign.

---

## 2. The dark + cute contrast works

Nobody complained about it.

Instead they described it as:

* unsettling
* interesting
* memorable

Meaning the contrast is functioning exactly as intended.

---

## 3. Story comes first

Multiple people immediately wanted:

* lore
* motivations
* relationships

rather than asking for visual tweaks.

That suggests the characters already invite curiosity.

---

## 4. Human art should take over soon

Almost everyone indirectly arrived at:

AI is useful for ideation.

The final identity should come from an artist.

---

## 5. Gameplay should reward exploration

Only Pyroxity discussed gameplay in detail, but the idea fits well with your overall direction:

* optional paths
* meaningful exploration
* replayability
* player expression

---

# Action items

## Character design

* Refine lore for clothing choices.
* Define why Patches and Vesper visually differ.
* Preserve the dark/cute contrast.

---

## Artist collaboration

* Present references, not finished designs.
* Encourage artistic interpretation.
* Ask which concepts excite the artist most.

---

## Gameplay

Investigate:

* branching exploration
* optional unlocks
* NPC-specific rewards
* alternate progression routes

---

## Narrative

Expand:

* Patches' engineering role.
* Vesper's philosophical role.
* Their relationship.
* Why they remain positive figures inside a hostile world.

---

# Overall conclusion

The feedback was remarkably consistent.

People were less interested in debating individual visual details than they were in discussing who these characters are and how they fit into the world.

That suggests the core concept is already succeeding.

The next major step is no longer generating additional AI concepts, but working with your artist to transform these ideas into a distinctive visual identity while simultaneously building gameplay and story around them.
